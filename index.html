What it is

A stethoscope for electric motors

MotorEar mounts on the motor housing with a magnet. Its sensors capture the vibration and sound of the motor, the device analyzes it, and predicts which component is wearing out long before it fails.

Listen

Attaches to the motor and records its vibration and sound.

Analyze

Breaks the signal into frequencies and finds the typical fault signatures of bearings, gearboxes and inverters.

Predict

Estimates remaining life and warns you about a failing component weeks before the breakdown.

How it works

Four steps, under a minute

Attach

Place MotorEar on the motor housing. The neodymium magnet holds it firmly.

Record

Press start. The device records the vibration and acoustic spectrum for a few seconds.

Analyze

MotorEar compares the sound with a healthy baseline and earlier measurements.

Predict

You get a clear status and an estimate of how long the component will last.

How the probe works

The probe is the part that actually touches the machine. You place it on the engine, gearbox or motor housing, and its strong magnet holds it firmly in place. No tools, no screws, no setup.

  • Vibration sensor. Every motor shakes in its own rhythm. When a bearing wears out, a part comes loose or something is out of balance, that rhythm changes long before you can hear it. The probe feels these tiny changes directly through the metal.
  • Microphone. At the same time, a built-in microphone listens to the sound of the engine from up close: knocks, whines, rattles and hisses.
  • Protected from outside noise. The sensors sit in a soft silicone layer inside a sealed aluminium capsule, so street noise, wind and voices don't get in the way. Water and dust stay out too.
  • Clean signal. A shielded cable carries the signal to the device without interference, and the quick-release connector lets you detach the probe in a second.

MotorEar combines what the probe feels and what it hears, compares it with how a healthy machine should sound, and shows you the result as a simple traffic light in the app.

Connectivity

OBD-II Bluetooth adapter

A compact MotorEar adapter plugs into a vehicle's standard OBD-II port and connects over Bluetooth, adding engine data to the acoustic analysis.

App

Wherever there's a motor, MotorEar hears it

Cars, trucks, tractors, agricultural machinery, machine tools and industrial equipment. The MotorEar app turns what the device hears and reads into a clear answer anyone can understand, no mechanical knowledge needed.

Machine→MotorEar device→MotorEar app

The device connects to the vehicle's OBD-II port through its own encrypted MotorEar adapter and sends everything to your phone over Bluetooth. The link is protected, so third-party adapters can't connect to it.

A traffic light, not error codes

Green means it's working fine. Yellow means check soon. Red means urgent, act now. Cryptic OBD-II error codes become one clear colour.

Exactly which part

Not just "something is wrong". The app points to the specific component, for example which bearing is wearing out, and highlights it on a diagram.

The person behind MotorEar

Built so that anyone can understand their machine

MotorEar was created by Roman Yashaev in Israel. It started with a simple question: why does it take an expert to know that something is wrong with a motor, when the motor is already telling us through its sound and vibration?

Roman's goal is to make professional-level diagnostics affordable and available to everyone: drivers, farmers, mechanics, workshop owners and factory staff. Above all, MotorEar is designed for people with no technical background. You don't need to read charts, know part numbers or understand engineering. You attach the probe, open the app and get a clear answer in plain words: green, yellow or red, which part, and what to do next.

Every detail of the device, from the large screen to the simple traffic-light result, follows one idea: technology should do the hard work, so people don't have to.

“I want every person, not only engineers, to be able to hear what their machine is saying and fix a problem before it becomes a breakdown.”
Roman Yashaev, founder
